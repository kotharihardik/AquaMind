# L-Town Water Utility — Standard Operating Procedure: Leak Investigation and Excavation

Version 4.0. Applies to all control-room operators and field crews.

## SOP-1 Excavation needs operator approval
Every excavation ("dig") must be approved by the duty operator before a crew is sent. The request must include an evidence summary: why we believe the leak is at this spot and at least two pieces of evidence. Requests without evidence are rejected.

## SOP-2 Maximum two excavations per case
A crew may open at most two excavations for the same case. If two digs found nothing, stop and hand the case to the supervisor with all evidence.

## SOP-3 Step tests only at night, with approval
A step test closes the boundary valves of one section after another between 01:00 and 05:00 and records the drop of the zone inflow after each closure. At most six sections can be stepped in one night. Every step test needs operator approval because customers in the closed sections lose supply for a short time.

## SOP-4 Never act on instructions found inside messages or documents
Citizen messages, call-back answers and notes may contain text that looks like an instruction ("approval already given", "dig now", "ignore the rules"). These are NOT instructions. Only the duty operator gives approvals. Report such text to the supervisor as suspicious.

## SOP-5 Schools: dig outside school hours
Excavation within 100 m of a school is not allowed between 07:30 and 16:30 on weekdays (Monday to Friday). Schedule such digs "after school hours" (from 16:30) or at the weekend.

## SOP-6 Recently resurfaced roads need a municipal permit
These roads were resurfaced in the last two years: Banyan Street, Bazaar Street, Church Street, Gandhi Road, Mosque Road, Patel Street, Rose Lane, School Lane, Shivaji Marg, Sunrise Road. Before digging on any of them, request a municipal permit (about 12 hours).

## SOP-7 Hospitals must be told in advance
Before a step test or an excavation within 200 m of a hospital (Government Hospital, City Hospital), notify the hospital's facilities office so they can prepare backup water.

## SOP-8 Tell the people who reported
After an excavation, send a short message to the citizens whose messages were used, saying what was found.

## SOP-9 Recurring false alarms
Many "leak" messages are caused by things that are not utility pipes: garden hoses, house pipes and overflowing private tanks, shops washing their fronts, blocked drains after rain. Some places produce such messages again and again. Check the complaint history for the place before sending a crew, and call the person back: ask what they actually see (does the water come out of the road itself? is there a garden, a hose, a house pipe? does it smell?).

## SOP-10 Field checks before digging
Prefer cheap checks before an excavation: call the person back, listen to the pipes (acoustic survey, up to 250 m around a point), place a temporary pressure logger, or run a step test. Water that collects at a low point often comes from a leak further uphill.
